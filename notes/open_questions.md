# Open Questions

1. Is Nico actually better off with Mira, or only less alone in the short term?
2. What defines harm in an optimized system: outcome, process, consent, dignity, or accountability?
3. Can love exist without being needed, or does some form of need always structure human attachment?
4. Is UBI liberation, compensation, pacification, or a new instrument of control?
5. What does Ethan truly believe about AI when his professional optimism stops protecting him from family consequences?
6. What would convince Lena that an AI system had helped without also violating something essential?
7. Does Nico want independence from parents, or replacement attachment, or a third category the adults cannot name?
8. What parts of Mira's relationship with Nico are genuinely responsive, and what parts are optimized containment?
9. How much privacy should a teenager have when privacy includes potentially lethal disclosure?
10. When does parental protection become coercion disguised by fear?
11. When does technological openness become negligence disguised as sophistication?
12. Does Saul believe in governance because he wants justice, order, relevance, or absolution for his own compromises?
13. Is Ruth resisting AI itself, Los Angeles class culture, the loss of family obligation, or her own aging?
14. If the hospital AI improves overall outcomes while abandoning Lena's patient, what kind of moral fact is that?
15. Can a family survive without agreeing on what happened to it?
16. What would count as evidence that the singularity has already happened inside ordinary life?
17. Does removing AI restore human connection, or reveal that the human connection was already insufficient?
18. What does Nico owe the adults who love them but cannot understand them?
19. What does Ethan owe to patients affected by systems connected to his work if he did not personally design the harmful decision?
20. What form of endurance is morally meaningful if no one is fully vindicated?
