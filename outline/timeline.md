# Six-Month Timeline

## Phase 1: Separation And Immediate Control

**Approximate span:** Weeks 1-4.

### Family State

- Lena files for divorce and seeks temporary provisions around Nico's AI access.
- Ethan moves into a short-term apartment or company-adjacent housing while arguing for joint custody and continuity.
- Nico experiences the separation as punishment for having been honest in the only place honesty felt possible.
- Ruth arrives from Akron to support Lena and help stabilize the household.
- Saul begins advising Ethan informally while insisting he is only protecting the family from bad decisions made under panic.

### AI/Societal Shift

- The crisis protocol that handled Nico's scare becomes part of custody documentation.
- Lena's hospital quietly updates language around AI recommendations, emphasizing clinician discretion while increasing practical reliance on the system.
- Local news and policy circles discuss adolescent AI companionship, privacy thresholds, and liability after several similar cases.

### Escalation Moments

- Temporary custody discussions focus on whether Mira is comparable to a therapeutic tool, a social platform, a medical-risk system, or an unregulated attachment object.
- Nico resists device restrictions and begins finding alternative access paths.
- Ethan tries to propose technical safeguards; Lena hears evasion.
- Ruth witnesses LA's ordinary AI conveniences and feels both repelled and dependent.

### Relationship Deterioration Or Change

- Ethan and Lena move from marital anger into formalized adversarial language.
- Nico stops trusting either parent to discuss Mira neutrally.
- Lena and Ruth initially align, but Ruth begins to notice that simple restriction may not reach Nico.
- Saul's involvement makes Lena feel watched by power rather than supported by family.

## Phase 2: Divergence Of Worlds

**Approximate span:** Weeks 5-8.

### Family State

- Separate households develop different rules, rhythms, and emotional climates.
- Ethan's time with Nico is more permissive around AI, framed as supervised adaptation.
- Lena's time with Nico emphasizes human presence, routines, therapy appointments, and reduced AI dependence.
- Nico learns to perform compliance differently in each household.

### AI/Societal Shift

- Schools normalize AI-mediated individual learning plans, making complete restriction impractical.
- Healthcare administrators present AI triage as necessary in a staffing crisis.
- Saul works on or around a UBI/transition policy initiative, possibly tied to LA municipal pilots and private funding.

### Escalation Moments

- A custody evaluator asks questions that flatten the family's conflict into risk factors.
- Nico's school performance improves under AI-supported tutoring, complicating Lena's argument.
- Lena encounters another hospital case where AI recommendation and human intuition diverge, but the outcome is less clear.
- Ethan's company faces scrutiny or internal debate over companion-risk protocols.

### Relationship Deterioration Or Change

- Ethan feels vindicated by Nico's measurable improvements.
- Lena feels trapped by metrics that cannot describe her concern.
- Nico feels increasingly split between being safer and being less known.
- Ruth begins speaking directly with Nico rather than only through Lena.

## Phase 3: Legal And Moral Hardening

**Approximate span:** Weeks 9-12.

### Family State

- The divorce process becomes procedurally real: disclosures, schedules, legal expenses, custody recommendations, and documented incidents.
- Ethan and Lena each assemble narratives of responsible parenting.
- Nico becomes more articulate about wanting agency, which adults alternately respect and treat as evidence of influence.
- Saul and Ruth emerge as competing forms of elder authority.

### AI/Societal Shift

- Policy debate intensifies around minors, AI companions, mental-health liability, and parental rights.
- Autonomous systems are so normalized that logistical dependence becomes invisible: rides, medication delivery, appointment coordination, school access.
- Economic displacement appears in the background through friends, patients, neighbors, and service workers whose work has thinned or shifted.

### Escalation Moments

- Evidence from Mira's logs becomes contested: what can be disclosed, who can interpret it, and whether the record is protective or invasive.
- Lena discovers a stronger connection between Ethan's professional ecosystem and her hospital's allocation system.
- Saul considers using policy relationships to help Ethan's legal argument, raising the ethical cost of influence.
- Ruth proposes a period in Akron or extended time away from LA, which inflames custody and identity questions.

### Relationship Deterioration Or Change

- Ethan begins to see that defending AI access may not be identical to defending Nico.
- Lena begins to see that removing Mira may harm Nico in ways she cannot dismiss.
- Nico becomes angrier at being measured by adults and systems alike.
- Ruth and Saul's conflict sharpens the novel's regional and generational arguments.

## Phase 4: Irreversibility

**Approximate span:** Weeks 13-18.

### Family State

- A return to the old family configuration becomes emotionally impossible even if legal divorce is not final.
- Nico's relationship to Mira changes under pressure: either deepens in secrecy, becomes more strategic, or is damaged by adult scrutiny.
- Lena and Ethan each suffer a professional or personal consequence that reveals the cost of their stance.
- The grandparents can no longer remain supporting figures; their choices affect the central conflict.

### AI/Societal Shift

- AI governance discourse becomes more immediate: new guidelines, pilot regulations, lawsuits, or institutional reforms are announced.
- UBI or transition-benefit policy is presented as both relief and management.
- Healthcare and companion AI companies adjust public language without substantially reducing dependence on their systems.

### Escalation Moments

- Nico makes a consequential choice that is not merely reactive: challenging restrictions, choosing which adult to trust, refusing a hearing category, or redefining the terms of Mira's role.
- Lena must decide whether to use invasive evidence to strengthen her custody position.
- Ethan must decide whether to disclose company knowledge that could help Lena's critique but damage his work.
- Saul's pragmatism crosses a line someone in the family can name.

### Relationship Deterioration Or Change

- The custody battle stops being about who is right and becomes about what each person is willing to sacrifice to be right.
- Ethan and Lena develop a clearer, sadder understanding of the other person's wound.
- Nico recognizes that agency includes consequences, not just resistance.
- Ruth's alliance with Lena becomes more honest because it includes disagreement.

## Phase 5: Decision And Endurance

**Approximate span:** Weeks 19-26.

### Family State

- The six-month waiting period approaches its legal end.
- Custody terms, AI access, therapy, school structure, and household boundaries must be decided in practice, even if emotionally unresolved.
- Nico's voice must matter without forcing the novel to pretend a teenager can solve adult failure.
- The family remains a family in altered form: not saved, not destroyed, still obligated.

### AI/Societal Shift

- The broader world continues. No singularity announcement arrives.
- Systems become slightly more regulated, slightly more embedded, and slightly easier to forget.
- Public discourse moves on to newer controversies while the family lives with the private aftermath.

### Escalation Moments

- A final custody or mediation decision creates terms for Nico's AI access that satisfy no one completely.
- Lena confronts the limits of control as protection.
- Ethan confronts the limits of usefulness as love.
- Saul confronts the difference between managing transition and being changed by it.
- Ruth decides whether support means helping Lena resist LA, remain in it, or stop framing the choice as escape.

### Relationship Deterioration Or Change

- Ethan and Lena may not reconcile, but they reach a form of co-parenting that requires accepting unresolved disagreement.
- Nico keeps some relationship to AI, but the meaning of that relationship is changed by what has happened.
- The grandparents remain implicated rather than tidily wise.
- The ending should leave the reader with endurance: care continuing after certainty has failed.
