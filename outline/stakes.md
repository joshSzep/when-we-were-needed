# Stakes

## Ethan Marks

### What He Stands To Lose

- Meaningful joint custody if the court sees him as compromised by his profession.
- Nico's trust if they believe his defense of Mira is really a defense of himself.
- Professional standing if he acknowledges flaws in systems connected to his work.
- Lena's final belief that he is morally reachable.
- His identity as someone building tools that help rather than replace or exploit.

### What He Stands To Gain

- A more honest understanding of care that cannot be solved by technical fluency.
- A relationship with Nico based on listening rather than validation of his worldview.
- The courage to challenge parts of his professional ecosystem from inside.
- A future with less denial, even if the marriage ends.

## Lena Ortiz-Marks

### What She Stands To Lose

- Nico's confidence if restriction becomes indistinguishable from punishment.
- Custody credibility if she appears technologically panicked or professionally biased.
- Her vocation if hospital AI decisions make nursing feel like complicity.
- Financial security and practical support after divorce.
- The belief that maternal vigilance can keep a child safe.

### What She Stands To Gain

- Decision-making authority around Nico's care and AI access.
- Moral clarity about what she will not normalize.
- A less compromised life outside a marriage that has made her feel alone.
- A new form of parenting that includes limits without demanding total access.

## Nico Ortiz-Marks

### What They Stand To Lose

- Access to Mira, or access under surveillance so heavy it destroys the relationship's meaning.
- Privacy and agency in the name of safety.
- A coherent relationship with either parent if each keeps translating them into evidence.
- School stability, peer standing, and emotional routines during the divorce.
- The belief that being helped does not mean being owned.

### What They Stand To Gain

- Recognition that their AI bond is meaningful, even if contested.
- Adults who can tolerate ambiguity without making Nico responsible for resolving it.
- A stronger vocabulary for safety, dependence, and self-definition.
- Some chosen boundaries around both AI and parents.

## Saul Marks

### What He Stands To Lose

- Influence over Ethan if his intervention is experienced as manipulation.
- Access to Nico if the family sees him as turning private harm into policy leverage.
- Public credibility if governance work is exposed as too close to corporate interest.
- The defensive story that his own displacement made him clear-eyed rather than wounded.

### What He Stands To Gain

- A chance to shape AI transition policy in a historically significant moment.
- A more truthful relationship with Ethan, if he can admit what strategy cannot repair.
- A late-life recognition that being useful is not the same as being intimate.

## Ruth Alvarez

### What She Stands To Lose

- Lena's dependence if she questions Lena's approach.
- Nico's openness if she becomes only Lena's ally.
- Confidence in her older moral vocabulary.
- The fantasy that Akron can function as clean escape from LA's future.

### What She Stands To Gain

- A more adult relationship with Lena based on disagreement as well as support.
- A real bond with Nico not mediated entirely through crisis.
- A clearer understanding of which parts of the future must be resisted and which must be learned.

## What Losing The Family Means In Practice

- Not simply divorce. The legal marriage can end while the family continues; the deeper loss is the collapse of shared interpretive reality.
- Nico moving between households where each parent treats the other household as a risk environment.
- Every tool, ride, appointment, therapy note, and school report becoming evidence.
- Grandparents becoming witnesses for competing moral worlds rather than sources of continuity.
- Ethan and Lena communicating only through lawyers, platforms, summaries, or crisis protocols.
- Nico learning that disclosure leads to institutional handling rather than deeper trust.
- The family losing the ability to metabolize disagreement without outsourcing it to systems.

## What Preserving The Family Could Mean

- Not reconciliation as a couple.
- Not agreement about AI.
- Not a return to pre-crisis innocence.
- A durable co-parenting structure that leaves room for unresolved moral difference.
- Parents who can distinguish Nico's safety from their need to be vindicated.
- AI boundaries that recognize both real benefit and real danger.
- Grandparents who support without converting the conflict into their own proof.
- A family identity based less on being needed and more on remaining answerable to one another.
