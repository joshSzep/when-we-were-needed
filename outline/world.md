# World

## Los Angeles, Roughly Five Years Ahead

### Autonomous Systems

- Waymo-like autonomous transport is ordinary across much of Los Angeles, though unevenly distributed by neighborhood, subscription tier, disability access, and municipal partnerships.
- Autonomous vehicles are not futuristic spectacle. They are school pickup options, late-shift transport, clinic shuttles, elderly mobility, delivery infrastructure, and custody-log evidence.
- Human driving still exists, but it increasingly carries class, temperament, or hobby meanings.
- Traffic has not disappeared. It has become more managed, more surveilled, and more difficult to narrate as anyone's fault.

### AI In Healthcare

- Hospitals use AI for triage, discharge planning, staffing predictions, compliance risk, charting, translation, insurance preparation, and patient monitoring.
- Clinicians are told AI recommendations are advisory, but institutional pressure makes deviation costly, slow, and legally exposed.
- Some patients benefit: earlier warnings, fewer medication errors, better translation, faster navigation through complex systems.
- Others experience dehumanization: risk scores replacing witness, discharge pathways framed as inevitability, and suffering translated into probabilities.
- Nurses carry the burden of explaining decisions they did not make and may not agree with.

### AI In Daily Decision-Making

- AI companions, tutors, schedulers, eldercare monitors, workplace copilots, legal-prep tools, and mental-health interfaces are widely normalized.
- Most people no longer ask whether AI should be involved; they ask which plan, which platform, which privacy setting, which liability waiver.
- Human relationships are increasingly coordinated through AI: apologies drafted, calendars negotiated, conflicts summarized, needs anticipated.
- The strangeness is normalized. People are unsettled less by AI's presence than by moments when it fails, withdraws, or reveals how much it knew.

### Economic Shifts

- White-collar status is less secure. Legal assistants, coders, schedulers, designers, analysts, support staff, and junior clinicians face compressed ladders.
- Service work remains, but often under algorithmic management or with reduced bargaining power.
- New work exists around oversight, prompting, compliance, data stewardship, human presence services, and bespoke analog experiences, but access is unequal.
- UBI and transition-benefit pilots are politically live, framed as liberation by some and containment by others.
- People who benefit from AI often describe displacement as temporary adjustment. People who lose roles experience it as identity collapse.

### Cultural Tone: Normalized Strangeness

- LA feels like the future because it has metabolized contradiction: spiritual wellness beside predictive policing, bespoke human therapists beside AI companions, climate anxiety beside luxury adaptation, labor politics beside automation capital.
- People speak fluently about mental health, optimization, access, safety, and burnout while remaining lonely and overmanaged.
- The city is not dystopian. It is functional enough to be seductive and unequal enough to be morally exhausting.
- The singularity is experienced as argument fatigue. Everyone has a position; almost no one knows what would count as proof.

## Akron Contrast

### Emotional And Cultural Gap

- Akron is not simply behind Los Angeles. It has different assumptions about obligation, pace, privacy, class visibility, and what counts as help.
- AI is present there too, but less glamourized. It appears through hospital networks, insurance systems, school platforms, logistics warehouses, municipal underfunding, customer service, and job displacement.
- People are less likely to describe technological dependence as lifestyle and more likely to encounter it through scarcity, closures, waiting lists, and outsourced decisions.
- Ruth's Akron perspective values showing up, calling directly, knowing who someone's people are, and distrusting systems that make refusal impossible.

### Not Less Advanced, But Different Values

- Akron may use fewer visible autonomous conveniences, but its residents understand displacement because they have lived industrial loss, institutional retreat, and promises of renewal before.
- The gap is not intelligence or sophistication. It is the difference between a city that markets transition and a city that remembers being transitioned upon.
- Ruth's critique of LA is partly accurate: LA can aestheticize surrender as progress.
- Ruth's critique is also limited: older communities could be cruel to difference, privacy, queerness, mental illness, and ambition.

## Background Institutions

### Family Court

- Family court has begun encountering AI companionship in custody disputes without stable precedent.
- Legal language struggles to classify companions: device, service, therapeutic aid, social contact, medical-adjacent tool, or private diary.
- Courts prefer risk categories. Nico's experience exceeds those categories.

### Schools

- Schools use AI tutors, behavioral analytics, adaptive grading, college-pathway planning, and social-risk alerts.
- Teachers vary: some depend on these systems to survive workload; others resent being turned into monitors of platform output.
- Students treat AI assistance as ordinary, but shame and status attach to which tools are permitted, customized, or paid for.

### Policy Space

- Governance discussions are crowded by corporate ethics teams, labor groups, municipal officials, parent advocates, healthcare systems, and philanthropic transition funds.
- UBI is debated as dignity, pacification, reparations, inflationary risk, and economic modernization.
- Saul's power lies in translating fear into policy language that funders and officials can use.
