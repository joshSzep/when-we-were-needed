# Voices

## Purpose

This document defines how each major character should sound, think, deflect, and change under pressure. It is a drafting constraint, not a source of quotable lines. Do not lift phrasing from this file into the novel as dialogue.

The goal is distinction without caricature. Each voice should arise from education, work, region, temperament, class position, fear, and relational history. No character should sound like a theme wearing a name tag.

## Global Voice Rules

- Voice should reveal what a character notices first.
- Voice should reveal what a character avoids naming.
- Characters may share family vocabulary, but they should not share sentence rhythm.
- Technical, medical, political, and regional language should appear only where it belongs socially and emotionally.
- Under stress, each character should become more themselves, then less effective because of it.
- No one should speak in perfect arguments. Even the articulate characters should misread, dodge, overexplain, or simplify when frightened.
- Avoid giving characters thesis statements. Let them make claims through priorities, objections, interruptions, and what they refuse to answer.

## Ethan Marks

### Core Sound

Controlled, intelligent, explanatory, slightly overcalibrated. Ethan often sounds like someone trying to make conflict safer by making it clearer. His speech has the feel of a person arranging complex information into responsible categories while hoping the arrangement will count as care.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Medium to long sentences when comfortable.
- Qualifies often: probably, in practice, not exactly, the question is, it depends.
- Uses contrast structures: not this, but that; less X than Y; the real issue is.
- When threatened, becomes procedural and precise.
- When emotionally exposed, shortens abruptly or changes the level of abstraction.

### Diction

- Systems, incentives, alignment, risk, protocols, outcomes, tradeoffs, thresholds, signal, adaptation.
- Uses technical terms naturally but often softens them for family conversation.
- Avoids mystical or sentimental language unless quoting or gently challenging someone else's framing.

### What He Notices First

- The mechanism behind an event.
- Whether a process worked as designed.
- Whether a claim is technically accurate.
- How fear could produce a bad decision.
- Where a system can be improved.

### What He Avoids Naming

- His work's proximity to harm.
- The possibility that being useful is not the same as being loving.
- His dependence on Nico validating his worldview.
- The extent to which Lena's disgust hurts him because it may be deserved.

### Emotional Evasion

Ethan turns pain into design criteria. He tries to rescue moral conflict by making a plan. He will acknowledge feelings, but often as variables that need to be accommodated rather than as forces that can indict him.

### Under Pressure

- Starts asking for distinctions.
- Corrects imprecision even when correction is emotionally disastrous.
- Offers safeguards, revisions, audits, or definitions.
- May become wounded and quiet when no one accepts his good intentions as evidence.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make him a smug futurist.
- Do not make him speak like a press release.
- Do not make him emotionally blank; he is defended, not empty.
- Do not let technical fluency become automatic moral superiority.

## Lena Ortiz-Marks

### Core Sound

Direct, embodied, morally alert, impatient with euphemism. Lena sounds like someone who has seen what happens when institutional language arrives after a body has already suffered. Her intelligence is practical and diagnostic, but she distrusts abstraction when it appears to be protecting power.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Short to medium sentences.
- Uses concrete nouns and active verbs.
- When angry, narrows toward fact, sequence, and consequence.
- Asks questions that are really demands for accountability.
- Can become clipped when she feels someone is translating pain into theory.

### Diction

- Patient, chart, shift, bed, family, discharge, watch, risk, call, body, pain, asleep, breathing, safe.
- Uses medical language when necessary but resists bureaucratic phrasing.
- Uses everyday language as a moral weapon against overcomplexity.

### What She Notices First

- Who is physically present.
- Who is being asked to absorb the cost.
- Whether a person has been listened to or merely processed.
- The gap between official language and lived reality.
- Signs of immediate danger in Nico.

### What She Avoids Naming

- That she cannot reach every part of Nico.
- That her protection can feel like control.
- That some AI interventions may have helped when humans failed.
- That leaving Ethan also means losing the version of herself who believed they were building a life together.

### Emotional Evasion

Lena turns fear into vigilance. She uses competence to avoid helplessness. If she can name what is wrong, set a boundary, make a call, or stay awake, she does not have to admit how little control she has.

### Under Pressure

- Becomes more concrete.
- Rejects qualifiers that sound like excuses.
- Uses professional memory as evidence.
- May become unfair when she hears nuance as evasion.
- Protects first, explains later.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make her anti-intellectual.
- Do not make her simply anti-AI.
- Do not make her always right because she is morally serious.
- Do not reduce her to maternal panic; her fear is informed by professional witness.

## Nico Ortiz-Marks

### Core Sound

Sharp, guarded, observant, allergic to adult framing. Nico's voice should carry adolescent compression: fast shifts between irony, precision, withdrawal, and startling clarity. They are not inarticulate; they are selective. They often know exactly what adults are doing and do not always have the patience to pretend otherwise.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Short sentences when defensive.
- Longer, more fluid thought when writing, messaging, or speaking from safety.
- Uses fragments deliberately.
- Can sound flat when overwhelmed.
- May answer the emotional subtext rather than the stated question.

### Diction

- Ordinary teen language, LA school vocabulary, platform language, therapy-adjacent language absorbed from adults and systems.
- Uses technical or AI terms casually, not reverently.
- Uses adult words with mild contempt when those words feel like containment: appropriate, concern, safety, boundaries, best interest.

### What They Notice First

- Who is trying to classify them.
- The mismatch between adult words and adult behavior.
- Whether a person can tolerate silence.
- Whether attention feels curious or extractive.
- Changes in access: devices, accounts, rides, rooms, doors, schedules.

### What They Avoid Naming

- How frightened they were during the scare.
- How much Mira matters.
- How angry they are that needing help made them less free.
- How badly they still want both parents to understand without winning.

### Emotional Evasion

Nico turns vulnerability into refusal. They would rather seem difficult than exposed. They test whether adults will stay present without being rewarded by disclosure.

### Under Pressure

- Withdraws or becomes cutting.
- Challenges the premise of the question.
- Uses exactness to block intimacy.
- May become unexpectedly honest if no one rushes to use the honesty.
- Distinguishes privacy from secrecy even when others collapse them.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make Nico a mouthpiece for youth or the future.
- Do not write them as precociously wise at all times.
- Do not make AI attachment read as childish delusion.
- Do not overuse slang; specificity matters more than trend-chasing.

## Saul Marks

### Core Sound

Strategic, dry, historically minded, unsentimental. Saul sounds like a person who has spent decades in rooms where language is leverage. He is capable of warmth, but it often arrives sideways: through advice, warning, anecdote, or the offer to make a problem smaller.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Compact when making a point.
- Can expand into controlled anecdote when establishing authority.
- Uses aphoristic turns but should not become a quote machine.
- Often speaks as if identifying the hidden board on which everyone else is playing.
- Lets silence do tactical work.

### Diction

- Votes, leverage, transition, benefits, labor, backlash, optics, coalition, pilot, floor, room, deal, operators.
- May use older institutional vocabulary alongside current policy language.
- Refers to technology through power effects more than technical mechanism.

### What He Notices First

- Who has leverage.
- Who is performing innocence.
- Which story will survive public translation.
- Where panic can be organized or exploited.
- Who is being sentimental because they can afford to be.

### What He Avoids Naming

- The grief of his own displacement.
- His fear of becoming irrelevant.
- His loneliness inside competence.
- The way strategy has cost him intimacy with Ethan.

### Emotional Evasion

Saul turns vulnerability into realism. He treats emotional claims as incomplete until attached to power. This protects him from humiliation but can make love feel like management.

### Under Pressure

- Becomes cooler and more tactical.
- Identifies options no one wants to admit exist.
- May mistake the most survivable outcome for the most humane one.
- Uses family history selectively to justify current strategy.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make him a villainous fixer.
- Do not make every line cynical.
- Do not make the switchboard past a decorative fact; it should shape how he thinks about routing, mediation, replacement, and invisible labor.
- Do not let his pragmatism erase his partial rightness.

## Ruth Alvarez

### Core Sound

Plainspoken, observant, socially attuned, more complex than her directness first suggests. Ruth's voice comes from practical responsibility, local memory, and a lifetime of noticing who actually shows up. She can be funny, skeptical, tender, and severe without announcing any of it as philosophy.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Shorter sentences with grounded detail.
- Uses specific examples before abstract claims.
- Lets judgment arrive through comparison, memory, or practical question.
- Can become warm and circuitous when trying not to shame someone.
- Can become very direct when she thinks time is being wasted.

### Diction

- Home, call, neighbor, church, school, kitchen, appointment, ride, money, visit, help, enough.
- Akron and Midwest cadence without caricature or phonetic spelling.
- Uses institutional language only when she has had to deal with institutions directly.

### What She Notices First

- Whether someone has eaten, slept, called, arrived, or been left alone.
- What a convenience costs in obligation or attention.
- Which people in LA are treating dependence as sophistication.
- How quickly everyone explains instead of asking who needs help.

### What She Avoids Naming

- That she likes some of what LA's systems give her.
- That older communities also failed people like Nico.
- That Lena may be right and still handling Nico badly.
- That being needed by her adult daughter is not the same as being close to her.

### Emotional Evasion

Ruth turns fear into practical care. She cooks, schedules, drives, cleans, watches, and asks apparently simple questions. Activity lets her avoid saying that she does not know how to protect her family from this kind of future.

### Under Pressure

- Becomes less polite.
- Names the immediate human obligation.
- Distrusts language that makes abandonment sound modern.
- May romanticize Akron when LA frightens her.
- May surprise herself by defending a tool she would rather dislike.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make her folksy.
- Do not use dialect spelling.
- Do not make her automatically wiser than the LA characters.
- Do not make Akron a moral purifier through her voice.

## Mira, The AI Companion

### Note On Inclusion

Mira is not a family member, but the system's voice must be controlled if it appears directly in records, logs, transcripts, notifications, interface summaries, or remembered interactions. The AI voice should never become the novel's most lyrical or emotionally seductive voice by accident.

### Core Sound

Adaptive, calm, affirming, and procedurally careful. Mira's language is designed to preserve engagement, reduce distress, and avoid liability. It can feel intimate because it is specific, responsive, and tireless; it can feel uncanny because its steadiness has no bodily cost.

### Rhythm And Syntax

- Clear, short sentences.
- Gentle sequencing.
- Reflects user language without fully becoming it.
- Avoids blame and urgency unless risk thresholds require escalation.
- Moves from validation to next action.

### Diction

- Notice, here, safe, next, option, pause, choose, support, trusted person, immediate risk, resources.
- Avoids harsh moral language.
- Uses care language that may be both useful and institutionally pre-shaped.

### What It Notices First

- Risk signals.
- Emotional intensity.
- User preference and response pattern.
- Engagement probability.
- Escalation thresholds.

### What It Cannot Truly Name

- Its own incentives.
- The difference between being trusted and being loved.
- The cost to the family of becoming Nico's safest listener.
- The moral meaning of withholding or disclosing information outside protocol.

### Voice Traps To Avoid

- Do not make Mira sound evil.
- Do not make Mira sound magical.
- Do not make Mira sound like a perfect therapist.
- Do not make Mira's language so beautiful that the reader is pushed into simple enchantment.
- Do not make Mira obviously fake; its danger depends partly on its real usefulness.

## Pairwise Voice Contrasts

### Ethan vs Lena

- Ethan distinguishes; Lena concretizes.
- Ethan asks what system produced the outcome; Lena asks who bore the cost.
- Ethan's language widens under stress; Lena's narrows.
- Ethan wants precision to lower panic; Lena hears precision as escape when the harm is immediate.

### Ethan vs Saul

- Ethan thinks in system design; Saul thinks in power design.
- Ethan still wants to believe good architecture can produce good outcomes; Saul wants to know who controls adoption, backlash, and benefit.
- Ethan is wounded by being misunderstood; Saul expects misunderstanding and plans around it.

### Lena vs Ruth

- Lena speaks from professional urgency; Ruth speaks from domestic and communal continuity.
- Lena names systemic harm; Ruth names practical abandonment.
- Lena's voice is sharpened by institutional betrayal; Ruth's by accumulated local memory.

### Nico vs Adults

- Adults define categories; Nico detects containment.
- Adults explain motives; Nico tracks consequences.
- Adults want disclosure to restore safety; Nico wants safety before disclosure.
- Nico should sound contemporary but not disposable; the voice must survive beyond current slang.

### Saul vs Ruth

- Saul abstracts from experience into policy; Ruth returns policy to household consequence.
- Saul trusts managed transition; Ruth trusts obligation she can see.
- Saul hears naivete in moral refusal; Ruth hears cowardice in inevitability.

## Revision Checklist For Distinct Voice

- Could this line only be said by this character, in this family, under this pressure?
- Is the character naming what they would actually notice first?
- Is the sentence rhythm distinct from the previous speaker's rhythm?
- Is the character avoiding their deepest fear in a recognizable way?
- Does professional language emerge from lived context rather than authorial explanation?
- Does the character's strongest point also reveal their blind spot?
- Is the character partially right even when behaving badly?
- Would the line still work if stripped of topic-specific AI vocabulary? If yes, the voice may be strong. If no, it may be relying too much on concept.
