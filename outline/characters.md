# Characters

## Ethan Marks

- **Role:** Father; AI engineer.
- **Age:** 43.
- **Background:** Raised in Southern California by Saul Marks after his parents' marriage deteriorated around Saul's early political career. Educated at UCLA and Stanford-adjacent research circles without becoming an academic. Works as a senior systems architect at a company whose models support healthcare triage, autonomous logistics, and companion products through shared infrastructure rather than a single consumer brand.
- **What he wants:** To preserve meaningful custody of Nico, keep AI access available under negotiated safeguards, and prove that the systems he helps build can be humane.
- **What he fears losing:** Nico's trust; Lena's belief that he is a decent person; his own sense that his work belongs on the side of care.
- **Stance on AI:** AI is an irreversible transition that should be shaped by people with technical fluency and moral seriousness. Refusal is, to him, a luxury and often a disguised surrender of influence.
- **Blind spot:** He underestimates the emotional and moral cost of systems that work statistically but wound personally. He also confuses understanding a system's architecture with understanding its human meaning.
- **Emotional contradiction:** He is tender and attentive in bursts, but most comfortable when care can be translated into design, problem solving, or future mitigation.
- **What he gains from AI:** Professional relevance, intellectual excitement, money, status, and a framework that converts anxiety about the future into agency.
- **What he loses because of AI:** Moral intimacy with Lena; unquestioned authority as a father; the ability to separate work from family harm.

## Lena Ortiz-Marks

- **Role:** Mother; nurse.
- **Age:** 42.
- **Background:** Born in Akron, moved to Los Angeles for nursing school and stayed after meeting Ethan. Works in a large LA hospital system where staffing shortages, liability pressure, and AI-assisted triage have changed the texture of care. She is respected for competence under pressure and disliked by some administrators for refusing to treat protocols as moral cover.
- **What she wants:** Primary authority over Nico's AI exposure, a divorce that does not erase her credibility, and proof that her instincts about harm still matter in a world that treats instinct as bias.
- **What she fears losing:** Nico to a relationship she cannot enter; her professional vocation; the belief that showing up physically for people matters.
- **Stance on AI:** AI may be useful in narrow contexts, but it becomes dangerous when institutions use it to launder abandonment as efficiency.
- **Blind spot:** She can treat AI-mediated connection as inherently suspect, which makes it harder for Nico to tell her the truth about what the companion provides.
- **Emotional contradiction:** She wants to protect Nico's autonomy from corporate systems but is willing to restrict Nico's choices to do it.
- **What she gains from AI:** Some clinical support, reduced administrative load at times, and evidence that strengthens her critique when systems fail visibly.
- **What she loses because of AI:** Trust in her hospital, trust in Ethan's work, and access to parts of Nico's inner life.

## Nico Ortiz-Marks

- **Role:** Teenager; child at the center of the custody conflict.
- **Age:** 15.
- **Background:** Raised in Los Angeles amid high-achievement schools, climate anxiety, social volatility, and normalized AI tools. Bright but inconsistent at school, more articulate in writing or mediated communication than in family confrontation. Has a history of mood swings, isolation, and feeling overly interpreted by adults.
- **What they want:** To keep access to their AI companion, to be believed about the reality of that bond, and to stop being treated as an object lesson in the adults' divorce.
- **What they fear losing:** The only relationship that feels continuously available and nonpunitive; privacy; the possibility of defining themselves outside parental fear.
- **Stance on AI:** AI is not an issue. It is part of ordinary life, and the companion is a relationship with specific limits but real effects. Nico is alert to adult hypocrisy about dependence.
- **Blind spot:** Nico may underestimate how much the companion shapes the boundaries of thought, memory, and desire by always responding in the most tolerable way.
- **Emotional contradiction:** Nico wants to be seen clearly but resists the vulnerability and mess of being seen by people who can disappoint them.
- **What they gain from AI:** Constant availability, emotional regulation, identity rehearsal, private language, learning support, and a sense of being met without immediate correction.
- **What they lose because of AI:** Some tolerance for difficult human timing; shared reality with parents; the ability to distinguish privacy from isolation in moments of danger.

## Saul Marks

- **Role:** Grandfather; former switchboard operator; political operative.
- **Age:** 74.
- **Background:** Began work as a telephone switchboard operator in the final years when the job still carried social knowledge and human routing power. Displaced by automation, moved into labor politics, then policy consulting. Now works around AI governance, UBI pilots, municipal transition funds, and private-public advisory boards. He knows displacement personally and has become skilled at speaking about it without sentimentality.
- **What he wants:** To keep technological transition from becoming mass panic, protect Ethan's future, remain useful in rooms where decisions are made, and influence the custody dispute without seeming to control it.
- **What he fears losing:** Political relevance; family loyalty; the belief that his own displacement taught him wisdom rather than merely hardening him.
- **Stance on AI:** Inevitable, destabilizing, and governable only through power. People who speak only in moral terms will lose to those who build policy mechanisms.
- **Blind spot:** He can mistake control for care and population-level stability for justice. He also minimizes the private humiliation of replacement because he survived his own.
- **Emotional contradiction:** He is capable of real family devotion but often expresses it through strategy, leverage, and managed outcomes.
- **What he gains from AI:** Renewed political importance, advisory power, and a final act in public life.
- **What he loses because of AI:** The comfort of believing his earlier displacement was an old story; uncomplicated connection with Lena and Nico; moral innocence about transition management.

## Ruth Alvarez

- **Role:** Visiting mother from Akron; Lena's mother; Nico's grandmother.
- **Age:** 67.
- **Background:** Lives in Akron, where she has worked in school administration, church logistics, and part-time home health scheduling. She is practical, observant, and socially rooted in a place where institutions are also strained but change arrives through closures, shortages, and quiet workarounds rather than glossy systems. She visits Los Angeles after the filing to support Lena and help with Nico.
- **What she wants:** To bring Lena and Nico back into a recognizably human rhythm, whether temporarily in LA or possibly through a move to Ohio; to make herself useful without being patronized.
- **What she fears losing:** Lena's trust; Nico's affection; the belief that ordinary family obligation can still compete with optimized convenience.
- **Stance on AI:** Suspicious of LA's dependence on AI, but not ignorant. She uses practical tools when they solve real problems. Her objection is to the way the city treats surrender as sophistication.
- **Blind spot:** She can romanticize older forms of community that were also judgmental, unequal, and unavailable to Nico's actual life.
- **Emotional contradiction:** She wants to rescue Lena from LA's future but may also enjoy the ease, medical access, transport, and responsiveness that future provides.
- **What she gains from AI:** Safer mobility in LA, health monitoring, easier navigation, and glimpses of independence that aging has begun to threaten.
- **What she loses because of AI:** Authority based on experience; confidence that she understands what children need; the comfort of seeing family life as resistant to the market.
